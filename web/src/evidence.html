Method

What is observed, what is modelled, what is assumed

observed Data

  • Rides between every pair of stops by hour: LTA DataMall fare-card OD, July and August 2026 (September as a check).
  • Bus routes, stop sequences, first and last buses, headway ranges: LTA DataMall (pinned copies in the repository).
  • Bus running times and headway regularity: — matched pairs of LTA's live, GPS-based arrival estimates, 15 Oct 2026, —.
  • Bus GPS movement: — pairs of reported positions of the same bus a few minutes apart.
  • Every Singapore bus ride (fare-card OD), to share Yishun's bus-km between internal and other riders (Part 2).
  • Roads: OpenStreetMap, pinned extract of 22 Oct 2026. Land use and boundaries: URA Master Plan 2019. Block storeys: HDB.

modelled Model

  • Road travel time = calibrated cruise time + — s per signal-controlled location; bus dwell — s per stop (+— s at stations). Same roads and speeds for both modes.
  • Bus wait = — × the combined headway of the lines serving a stop pair (common lines). A rider boards a looping line at whichever visit of the stop gives the shortest ride.
  • Shuttles: event-driven simulation, immediate insertion dispatch, rides within 1.6× direct + 4 min, idle vehicles reposition towards expected demand. A stop takes — s plus 4 s per rider (assumed).
  • Costs (Part 2): low, central and high values per parameter, from published contracts, prices and wages; vehicles bought or leased, never both.

assumed Not modelled

  • Demand held fixed: today's riders, times and stops; no fares, mode shift or induced trips.
  • No congestion feedback: hundreds of shuttles do not slow each other or the buses.
  • Bus crowding and denied boarding; riders timing their arrival with live bus information.
  • Charging (Part 2 estimates reserves, it does not simulate charging), breakdowns, staff relief, COE and registration taxes on vehicles.

What moves the answers

One assumption at a time

Part 1: the fleet that keeps up with the bus all day

Smallest fixed fleet of 10-seat shuttles that keeps up with the bus in every hour on all three simulated days, when one assumption changes. The base case is the vertical line; fleets are searched on a 25-vehicle grid.

Part 2: the cost of an hour of riders' time saved

Cost per passenger-hour saved when one cost parameter moves to its low or high value, the others central, for each staffing case, as a change from that case's central value. One axis for all three, so bar lengths compare.

Part 2 · independent review

Nine findings, and what was done about each

Part 2 was reviewed by a reviewer who had not built it, who read the code and outputs, recomputed costs by hand, rebuilt the summary and re-ran a recorded simulation. Every result on the study page comes from the runs made after these fixes.

StatusFindingWhat was done
fixedR1 · material. The bus model boarded riders at the first visit of a stop on a line. Feeder 812 is a figure of eight through Yishun Interchange, so a rider for its second loop was timed riding the first loop as well: today's bus times were too long on exactly the rides the combined service selects.Riders board at whichever visit gives the shortest ride, tested on a figure-of-eight line and checked against brute force on every real line that revisits a stop. Model, both parts' experiments and every table re-run.
fixedR2 · material. "The combined service buys time more cheaply than any full replacement" holds only with every bus kept.The claim is qualified, and full replacement with the internal-only services withdrawn is reported with its cost per hour saved.
fixedR3 · material. The stress tests compared two service designs: full replacement accepted 45-minute pickups with no bus to fall back on, the combined service declined after 15 minutes and the rider took the bus.Every stress now also runs full replacement under the combined service's rule; the base row uses the same three days as the stress rows; "realistic kerb limits" became test values.
statedR4. No rider times their arrival with live bus information.Added to the limits; the reviewer's first-order estimate is that savings fall by about a fifth if only bus riders did.
statedR5. No relief or spare staff; unlimited kerb in the base case.Added to the limits; a 10–15 % relief factor would add about 4–7 % to on-board costs without changing the ranking.
fixedR6. The full bus cost per bus-km may count LTA's assets twice.Avoidable bus cost reported at the full cost and at the operator fee only.
fixedR7. LTA's route, service and stop files were not pinned.Committed with checksums; make data restores them.
fixedR8. Report statements stronger than the outputs, one of them wrong (4-seat vehicles are also cheaper per hour with an operator on board).Report rewritten from the re-run results.
fixedR9. Defects in generated tables and charts (blank subzone names, an axis showing 7,500 as "8k").Fixed in the generators.

Reproduce

Rebuild every number from the raw data

With a free LTA DataMall key in LTA_DATAMALL_KEY. The live bus data of 15 October 2026 cannot be downloaded again, so the calibration fitted on it is committed and used; DataMall serves fare-card months for three months only, so the Yishun OD table is committed too.

make setup                    # Python 3.12 environment, pinned packages
make data                     # LTA, data.gov.sg; restores the pinned OSM and LTA bus files
make reproduce-core-results   # Part 1: experiments, sensitivity, page data, tests (about 2.5 h on 4 cores)
make phase2-allocation        # Part 2: who uses Yishun's bus-km (needs the July and August OD files)
make phase2-experiments       # Part 2: options A, B and C on shared simulated days (about 1.5 h)
make phase2-results           # Part 2: summary, page data, report charts and results tables
make web && make serve        # this site at http://localhost:8000
